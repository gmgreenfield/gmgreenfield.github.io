To keep my mind agile and my programming skills from getting rusty, I bought a membership to LeetCode. Did I mention that I suck at these coding puzzles? This is mostly a form of insurance: if I get an interview, I'll be more or less prepared.

Well... that, and keeping a blog maintained is an ordeal. Perhaps my ego just isn't inflated enough to convince me that I need to keep the onlookers updated. Kidding aside, I understand and accept that nobody is looking at this thing.

Anyway, $159 up front for a year seems okay for what you get. Some of the problem descriptions are worded a bit strangely (e.g., “non-decreasing array”—wait, you mean increasing?), but it’s certainly helping keep my skills in various languages from getting stale. As I wrote in an earlier entry, so many “programmers” are going to be useless meat puppets who couldn’t be bothered to look at the generated code. It’s going to be amusing even three years from now.

These large software companies will probably keep doing LeetCode-based hiring, if only because the engineer interviewing you did it, so it becomes a rite of passage.

Embarrassingly, though, some of the medium-difficulty problems are throwing me for more of a loop than I expected. For example, #55, “Jump Game”:

> You are given an integer array `nums`. You are initially positioned at the array's first index, and each element in the array represents your maximum jump length at that position.
>
> Return `true` if you can reach the last index, or `false` otherwise.
>
> **Example:**
>
> **Input:** `nums = [2,3,1,1,4]`
>
> **Output:** `true`
>
> **Explanation:** Jump 1 step from index 0 to 1, then 3 steps to the last index.

Of course, once you have the answer written out, it seems trivial. But getting there is the thought process you need to exercise. Thinking as an activity in itself is so underrated. Many people nowadays have let it die. Literally, if you don't use it, you lose it. A lot of people aren't going to have the faculties to reason through concepts like this. If you don't believe me, well, look at what our society has devolved into. Seriously.

All right, enough of the blackpill. It keeps me busy. One can only do so much mindless YouTube watching before turning into a boomer. My hope is to get so tuned in to the problem-solving process that each problem takes three minutes at most.
