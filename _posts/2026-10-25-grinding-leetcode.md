In order to keep my mind agile and to not loose any programming skill, I've bought a membership to leetcode.com. Did I mention that I suck at those coding puzzles? This is more of a sense of insurance that if I get an interview, I'll be more or less prepared.

Well... that and keeping a blog maintained is an ordeal: Perhaps my ego just isn't inflated enough at the moment to signify to myself that I must keep the onlookers updated. Kidding aside, I understand and accept that nobody is looking at this thing.

Anyways, the $159 upfront for a year is ok for what your able to get out of it. Some of problem descriptions are worded a bit strangely (e.g. non-decreasing array, wait you mean increasing?), but it's certainly helping to avoid skills in various
languages from getting stale. As I put in an earlier entry so many "programmers" are going to be useless meat puppets who couldn't be bothered to look at the generated code, it's going to be amusing even three years for now.

These large software companies likely are still going to be doing leetcode based hiring merely for the fact that the engineer interviewing you did it so it's going to be the right of passage sort of thing.

Embarrassingly though many of the medium difficulty ones are throwing me for a loop more than expected. For example, #55 "Jump Game":

>You are given an integer array nums. You are initially positioned at the array's first index, and each element in the array represents your maximum jump length at that position.
>
>Return true if you can reach the last index, or false otherwise.
>
>Example:
>
>Input: nums = [2,3,1,1,4]
>
>Output: true
>
>Explanation: Jump 1 step from index 0 to 1, then 3 steps to the last index.

Of course once you have the answer written out it's trivial, but getting there is the thought process to be fully exercised. Thinking as an activity in itself is so underrated, many people now days have let it die. Literally, if you don't use it you will loose it. Many people aren't going to have the faculties to reason about such concepts as this. If you don't believe me, well look at what our society has devolved into. Seriously.

Alright enough of the blackpill, it's keeping me busy. One can only do enough mindless YouTube watching before they end up like a boomer. My hope here is to be so tuned in my problem solving thought process is that each problem will take three minutes at most.
